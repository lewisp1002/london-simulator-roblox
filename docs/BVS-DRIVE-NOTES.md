# BVS drive-round, 17 Oct 2026 (watched live)

What we saw while the owner drove round Birmingham Vehicle Simulator, to guide the London rebuild.

## City centre (first 2 minutes)
- Roads: wide, 2 lanes plus room, grainy grey tarmac with visible texture. Lots of detailed paint: KEEP CLEAR,
  BUS GATE, BUS STOP (yellow), LOOK RIGHT on the pavement, 20 roundels, hatched areas, give-way triangles, turn arrows.
  Single yellow lines along kerbs. Few parked cars in the centre.
- Pavements: very wide, herringbone block paving (a real texture, red-grey), raised kerbs. Red-paved traffic islands.
- Trees everywhere: in square tree pits along the kerb every ~20 studs, in raised brick planters, and dense
  mesh trees behind walls. Hedges on central reservations.
- Furniture: black railings (curved round corners), black bollards, black traffic lights on grey poles, keep-left
  bollards, tall slim grey lamp posts with long arms, red phone boxes, bus shelters, bins, a cycle-hire dock, benches,
  billboards, construction hoardings (blue).
- Buildings: big and modern with REAL TEXTURES. Glass curtain-wall offices with mullion bands, dark-clad blocks,
  1960s concrete panel offices with realistic windows, a multi-storey car park, a supermarket with bold graphic
  cladding (parody brand), red-brick 3-4 storey warehouses and offices with photo-real brick and 6-pane sash
  windows, shops with parody names and window displays. Buildings come right up to the pavement.
- Landmarks you recognise: the Selfridges disc building (detailed mesh), Moor Street station with its lettering,
  Grand Central dome, St Martin's church spire, railway station platforms with trains. Open market stalls.
- Strong haze: distant towers fade to grey, so the city seems endless.
- Junctions are wide and open, with islands, lights at stop lines, lots of paint.

## Out of the centre (minutes 2-5)
- HILLS: roads climb and dip, run on embankments and in cuttings, with brick retaining walls and concrete
  parapets. Flyovers cross over roads; one road runs sunk below street level (blue railings, painted mural walls).
  Our map is completely flat: the biggest single difference.
- Woodland: dense blocks of tall pines (60-80 studs) and leafy trees behind railings and along roads; long grass
  verges (Terrain grass with blades). Shrubs and bushes everywhere, flower beds along railings.
- Dual carriageways: 2-3 lanes each way, turn-lane arrows, hedges or grass down the middle, green direction
  sign gantries, verges between road and pavement, painted blue cycle lanes with red/white wands.
- Big roundabouts with traffic lights on them and planted islands; chevron signs.
- Retail parks and trade counters (parody names), industrial estates of modern sheds with coloured bands,
  student towers in brick with strip windows, 1960s office slabs.
- Crossings: zig-zags, guardrails, LOOK markings. Repeater 30 signs on lamp posts. Roundels painted on the road.
- Road paint looks worn and textured, not bright solid white.

## Estates, shopping streets, industry (minutes 5-8)
- Council tower blocks stand in open parkland: wide lawns, scattered leafy trees, pines, footpaths. Not packed.
- Dual carriageways with a very wide grass central reservation (~40 studs) and grass verges both sides.
- Grade-separated junction: slip road loops down under a long road bridge with railings, brick retaining walls.
- Yellow box junctions, signalised junctions, big UK green direction signs with route numbers, H and P symbols.
- Shopping streets (Digbeth/Hockley style): cars parked both sides, 3-4 storey buildings with roller shutters and
  parody fascias, cream stucco with arched windows, Victorian red brick with tall multi-pane windows and cornices.
- Industrial estates: corrugated-clad sheds with big parody signage, full car parks, wide roads.
- Skyline landmarks seen from everywhere (the BT-style tower, church spire), so you always know where you are.
- City plazas with concrete anti-ram blocks, bollards with lights, landscaped banks with hedges.

## What makes BVS look real, and what ours lacks (in order of impact)
1. Photo-real textures on everything: brick, glass, concrete, block paving, worn road paint. Ours is flat colour.
2. Hills and levels: embankments, cuttings, flyovers, underpasses, slip roads under bridges. Ours is flat.
3. Greenery: woods of tall trees, shrubs, hedges, planters, long-grass verges, parkland round estates. Ours is
   mostly buildings and short lawns.
4. Mix of density: a dense modern centre (big offices, malls, stations, car parks), then open green outskirts.
   Ours packs every block with buildings everywhere, which looks fake and samey.
5. Wider roads: 2-3 lanes, verges, cycle lanes, signalised big roundabouts, yellow boxes, sign gantries.
6. Recognisable landmarks modelled in detail.
7. Lots of street detail: realistic signs, cycle docks, bollards, planters, railings, hoardings.
